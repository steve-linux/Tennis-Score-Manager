Tennis Score Manager keeps the score of a tennis match by the ITF rules and calls it out like a chair umpire.

* Singles and doubles: three sets with a tie-break at 6-6, or two sets and a 10-point match tie-break; optional No-Ad scoring.
* Padel too: same rules as tennis, with doubles and golden point already set; the FIP star point is also available.
* Chair umpire calls in six languages (Italian, English, French, German, Spanish, Portuguese) with the phone's text-to-speech, no internet needed. You can also import your own recordings.
* Shot clock (25 s), changeover (90 s) and set break (120 s); changes of ends and serving order are handled for you.
* Undo any point, even after the end of a game, set or match: the score is recomputed from the start.
* Optional M5StickS3 wristbands over Bluetooth LE: each player presses KEY1 to win the point and KEY2 to undo it. Their Arduino firmware is in the same repository. From firmware 2.4 they also recognize forehand, backhand, serve and volley after a short calibration.
* No wristbands? Two Bluetooth selfie remotes (€1–3 each), paired like a keyboard, do the job: one click = point, double click, long press or the other button = undo.
* LED-style scoreboard on a TV or monitor: a second phone with an HDMI cable, a Chromecast (screen cast), or any browser on the phone's hotspot.
* Match summary with the score set by set, duration, club, court and place; save it as text, JSON or image, or share it.
* End-of-match statistics, for the match or per set (serve, return, break points, points in a row…), and a point-by-point log with break points, set points and match points.
* Detailed stats, for someone scoring from the side of the court: aces, double faults, first serves, winners and errors.
* History on the phone: finished matches to reopen, players with wins, losses and head to head; matches saved (JSON) on another phone can be imported, also with a QR code on the same Wi-Fi. Player photos, on the TV scoreboard too.

No ads, no account, no tracking. The network is used only for the scoreboard and the transfer between phones, on the local network. The camera is used only to read the transfer QR code. Location is used for the place of the match in the summary and, as Android requires, for the Bluetooth search for the wristbands. The court address is looked up only if you turn on the option: it uses the phone's geocoding service, which with Google services sends the coordinates to Google.
